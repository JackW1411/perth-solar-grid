# Stage 1: Quantifying midday solar surplus

## Anchor figure

AEMO forecasts that minimum unscheduled operational demand in the SWIS falls from **319 MW in 2025-26 to 193 MW in 2028-29**, and to **50 MW by 2035-36** (50% POE, Expected scenario). Below the **300 MW minimum demand threshold**, AEMO must actively manage system security through market signals, battery charging or Emergency Solar Management.

AEMO: "Negative unscheduled operational demand would indicate periods where distributed PV generation exceeds operational demand, creating surplus energy for storage or curtailment."

Source: 2026 WEM ESOO, section 2.6 (pp. 48 to 51). See DATA_SOURCES.md.

## Supporting context

- Rooftop solar in the SWIS: 3.2 GW across about 0.6 million systems at end of Q2 2026, up from 1.6 GW in 2020 (QED Q2 2026, section 4.1.1).
- Grid batteries charge mainly in the middle of the day. Charging records of 1,011 MW and 1,031 MW in Q2 2026. Fleet of 1,275 MW / 5,140 MWh (QED Q2 2026, section 4.3).
- Battery discharge passed 1 GW in the evening peak in December 2025, and average gas-fired generation fell 126 MW (16.4%) year on year in Q4 2025 (QED Q4 2025, section 4.2).

## Method note

- AEMO does not publish a SWIS figure for curtailed or wasted rooftop solar energy. Curtailment figures in the QED are for the NEM and must not be quoted for WA.
- So the anchor is the minimum demand forecast against the 300 MW threshold, not a GWh volume. It measures how close midday demand gets to the point where surplus has to be stored or switched off.
- Forecasts are AEMO's Expected scenario at 50% POE. Minimum demand excludes utility-scale battery charging, so it shows the surplus before batteries absorb it.

## Implication for the project

Batteries are already absorbing midday surplus and displacing evening gas. The project asks where the next batteries add the most value as minimum demand keeps falling, not whether solar is being wasted today.
